001Philosophy & offering

The knowledge is proprietary. The structures are not.

First-loss tranches, advance market commitments, FX hedge facilities, parametric insurance, outcome bonds, tax credit stacking. None of it is exotic. The barrier is informational, not intellectual.

002The four dimensions

Every structure we build is optimized across four axes simultaneously.

Risk. Return. Liquidity. Impact. Most structuring tools optimize for one or two and treat the rest as constraints. We treat all four as design parameters — and the mark itself encodes that discipline.

An efficient structure is not the one that maximizes return alone. It is the one that pushes the frontier outward across all four dimensions for a given mandate — and that requires diagnosis before instruments.

003Methodology

Diagnostic first. Instruments second.

Most structuring fails at the same point: it reaches for instruments before diagnosing the constraint.

A practitioner who defaults to a first-loss tranche has not asked whether the binding problem is risk tolerance, revenue uncertainty, offtaker fragmentation, FX exposure, or timeline mismatch. Each requires a different instrument. Each carries different implications for leverage, exit, and the cost of concessional capital.

  1. STEP 01

    Diagnose the constraint

    Risk tolerance? Revenue uncertainty? Demand fragmentation? FX mismatch? Timeline? Market proof? Naming the binding constraint is the single decision that determines whether the rest of the work is leverage or theatre.

  2. STEP 02

    Map structures to constraints

    Draw from the full universe of available structures, not only familiar ones. Identify where instruments from adjacent sectors transfer — FX hedge logic from East African energy access maps directly to Southeast Asian green manufacturing.

  3. STEP 03

    Understand the exit

    Every instrument has exit implications. A first-loss layer that stays in place until loan repayment is a fundamentally different commitment than one that exits at financial close. Exit shapes the structure as much as entry does.

  4. STEP 04

    Model leverage and capital mobilization

    What does the structure unlock per dollar of concessional capital deployed? This determines whether the structure is genuinely additive or merely subsidized.

  5. STEP 05

    Stress test before close

    Bull and bear cases. Capital recycling implications. Credibility risks if the structure underperforms. The discipline most often skipped is the one that most often saves the deal.

004The framework

Constraint archetypes mapped to structural categories.

The intellectual core that the platform is built on, and that the design partnership uses every day.

Constraint archetypes

  • Risk tolerance — investor requires downside protection
  • Revenue uncertainty — project lacks creditworthy offtake
  • Demand fragmentation — no single buyer will anchor
  • FX mismatch — revenues and costs in different currencies
  • Timeline mismatch — development period exceeds fund life
  • Technology & development risk
  • Market proof for first‑of‑kind
  • Regulatory and tax credit complexity

Structural categories

  • Junior equity and first‑loss tranches
  • Guarantees and credit enhancement
  • Advance market commitments
  • Outcome bonds and milestone structures
  • Revenue‑based and royalty financing
  • Local currency and FX hedge facilities
  • LIHTC, NMTC, IRA tax credit stacks
  • Parametric insurance, escrow waterfalls

Deal-by-deal support

Portfolio strategy

For allocators building deliberate impact portfolios: we design portfolio strategies that are precisely oriented around your risk, return, liquidity, and impact targets. Impact SMA / external CIO
